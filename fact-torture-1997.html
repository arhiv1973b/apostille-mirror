👤 PRIMARY VICTIM

📖 DETAILED ACCOUNT

On 15 March 1997, Alexei Macheret was detained in a state facility in Chişinău. During detention, he was subjected to systematic torture and inhuman treatment in violation of fundamental international law.

Methods documented:

  • Physical violence and beatings
  • Sleep deprivation
  • Psychological torture and threats
  • Denial of medical care
  • Conditions amounting to cruel and degrading treatment

⚖️ INTERNATIONAL LAW VIOLATIONS

Jus Cogens Norms Breached

Convention Against Torture (CAT)
Article 1 & 4: States must prevent and punish torture under all circumstances
International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR)
Article 5: Prohibition of torture and cruel, inhuman, degrading treatment (non-derogable)
European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR)
Article 3: Absolute prohibition on torture (cannot be suspended even in emergency)
UN Convention Against Torture & Other Cruel, Inhuman or Degrading Treatment
Signature: CRITICAL proof that Moldova acknowledged this obligation
🔍 JUS COGENS STATUS
Jus cogens (peremptory norms of international law) are absolute and cannot be derogated from. The prohibition on torture is universally recognized as jus cogens — binding all states regardless of treaty ratification or state claims.

📌 STATE RESPONSIBILITY

The Republic of Moldova, as a state party to CAT and ICCPR, bears full responsibility for:

  • Absolute prohibition: Torture committed by state agents is prohibited without exception
  • Prevention duty: States must prevent torture from occurring
  • Investigation duty: Independent investigation is mandatory when torture allegations arise
  • Prosecution duty: Perpetrators must be prosecuted or extradited
  • Reparation duty: Victims have right to restitution and rehabilitation
⚠️ CRITICAL FINDING
The Moldovan state has failed in all these duties. The perpetrators remain unprosecuted, victims remain uncompensated, and the investigation was never conducted. This constitutes a continuing violation of international law.
