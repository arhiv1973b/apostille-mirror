# Historical Defect of ECHR: ETS nr. 2 Anchor

**JURISDICTIONAL BASELINE:** UDHR 1948 (Jus Cogens, Erga Omnes)
**CASE REFERENCE:** CASE-MACHERET-1997-2026
**EVIDENCE NODE:** S-22 Verdict (1998)

## 1. The Chronological Architecture of Immunities
The conventional legal framework contains an embedded structural defect designed to shield institutional apparatuses through procedural filters. 

* **1948 — UDHR (The Absolute Shield):** Establishes the imperative, non-derogable prohibition of torture without statutes of limitation.
* **1949 — ETS nr. 2 (Institutional Immunity):** General Agreement on Privileges and Immunities of the Council of Europe. Ratified by Moldova in 1997, immediately preceding the Macheret case. It creates a jurisdictional firewall protecting institutional actors.
* **1950 — ECHR (Procedural Bureaucratization):** Transforms the absolute imperative of human rights into a heavily filtered procedure, subordinating Jus Cogens to administrative admissibility criteria.

## 2. The S-22 Singularity (1998)
The judicial verdict S-22 legally fixed the fact of torture. Under Article 15 of the Convention Against Torture (CAT) and the peremptory norms of Jus Cogens, the fixation of this fact creates a legal singularity:
* It permanently pierces the ETS nr. 2 immunity firewall.
* It renders ECHR procedural filters (such as the 6-month rule or exhaustion of local remedies) legally void, as administrative rules cannot override Jus Cogens.

## 3. The Poisonous Tree Doctrine
Because the foundation (S-22) fixed the use of torture, all subsequent judicial acts—including the 2025 Appeals that utilized procedural forgery to ignore Art. 15 CAT—are legally null and void ("fruits of the poisonous tree"). ETS nr. 2 cannot be invoked to shield actors from continuing consequences of non-rehabilitation.
