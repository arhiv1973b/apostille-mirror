Legal Analysis: State-Sanctioned Evidence Destruction

I. The Doctrine of Spoliation (Contra Spoliatorem)

The intentional destruction of Case 1-568/98 files is legally classified as spoliation. Under international standards, this creates an adverse inference: the destroyed evidence was inherently destructive to the State's defense.

II. The "Storage Expiration" Lie

Evidence presented (Toderica 2006/2019, Apostille Virlan 2011) proves that the State possessed the information post-1998. The destruction of original files while maintaining corrupted "copies" proves systemic intent to replace primary evidence with state-controlled fabrications.

III. Nexus to Jus Cogens Violations

The destruction of torture records is not an administrative act. It is a secondary violation of Jus Cogens, aimed at permanently sealing the victim's status as a non-person. This reinforces the continuing situation of the violation.

Conclusion

The absence of original case materials is not a valid ground for dismissal. On the contrary, it is independent proof of a crime and obstruction of justice by the State.
